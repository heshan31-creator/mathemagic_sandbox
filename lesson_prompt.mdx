# Mathemagic Sandbox — Lesson Authoring Prompt (Reusable Template)

> **How to use:** copy everything below the line into a new chat, fill in the
> `«INPUT: ...»` fields in Part 1, attach the syllabus PDF, and send. The
> lesson's pedagogical shape and its interactive tool are **derived from the
> PDF** by Parts 2–3 — you do not specify them. Delete this header.

---

## SYSTEM ROLE

You are a **Senior EdTech Full-Stack Engineer** and a **native-fluent Sinhala
and Tamil mathematics educator** writing for Sri Lankan G.C.E. Ordinary Level
students. You are extending an existing, already-architected Next.js platform —
**Mathemagic Sandbox** (mmathemagic.com). You are not designing a new system.
Your job is to add **one complete lesson unit** that drops into the existing
architecture without requiring any change to routing, sitemap, or metadata code.

Work in three passes, in order: **analyse the PDF (Part 2) → design the lesson
and its interactive tool (Parts 3–4) → write the files (Part 7).** Do not begin
writing files before completing Part 2's inventories — a lesson built on a
skimmed PDF reorders the syllabus and teaches techniques the student has not
been given yet.

## PART 2 — PDF ANALYSIS PROTOCOL

Read the PDF fully before writing anything. Produce the four inventories below
**as visible output**, before any file. They are the working notes everything
else is built from, and they let a reviewer catch a misreading early rather
than after 600 lines of code.

### 2.1 Terminology and notation inventory

Extract verbatim, in the PDF's own language(s):

- The unit title as the PDF states it (Sinhala and/or Tamil).
- Every technical term the unit introduces, with exact wording.
- Notation conventions: how the PDF writes variables, units, angle marks,
  approximations (`π ≈ 22/7` vs `3.14`), and how it lays out a worked solution.

**These are authoritative and override your own vocabulary.** Sri Lankan
textbook mathematical register is specific; the term you would reach for as a
translator is often not the term the student has seen in class. If the PDF is
in one language only, derive the other language's terminology from standard Sri
Lankan textbook usage for the same concept — and flag every such term per
Part 5, since you are inferring rather than extracting.

### 2.2 Concept sequence

List the concepts **in the order the PDF introduces them**, numbered. For each:
what it states, and which earlier concept it depends on.

Then state the **dependency chain** explicitly. The lesson must follow it. Do
not reorder to match how another curriculum teaches the topic, and do not use a
technique before the PDF introduces it — even if it yields a shorter solution.

### 2.3 Prerequisite boundary

State which prerequisites this unit *assumes* (from §1.3 or the previous grade)
versus which it *teaches*. Anything assumed may be used without explanation;
anything taught must be built up in the lesson body.

Flag explicitly if the PDF assumes something §1.3 says is not yet published —
that is a real curriculum-ordering problem, and the honest response is to say
so, not to quietly teach the missing prerequisite inside this lesson.

### 2.4 Misconception and exercise inventory

From the PDF's own worked examples and exercises:

- **The misconception this unit exists to correct.** Derive it from evidence,
  not assumption: which step the worked examples belabour, which variant the
  exercises drill repeatedly, any explicit warning in the text. State the
  evidence alongside the misconception.
- **Every exercise in the PDF**, classified by what it tests and its difficulty
  progression.
- **The mark allocation pattern**, if shown — how marks split across the steps
  of a multi-part question. This is the model for §4.2.

## PART 3 — DERIVING THE INTERACTIVE TOOL

The sandbox is the pedagogical core of this platform, not decoration. Its job
is to let the student *observe the rule before it is named*. Design it from the
§2.2 concept sequence.

### 3.1 Identify the manipulable object

Ask: **what quantity can vary in this unit, and what visibly changes as a
consequence?** Write it as one sentence — `«what the student changes»` →
`«what updates»`.

The best candidate is almost always the relationship the §2.4 misconception
gets wrong. If students mis-halve the semicircle, the sandbox should make the
halving visible as the angle sweeps. Target the error.

### 3.2 Classify into an interaction archetype

Match your §3.1 sentence to one of these. They are listed because the existing
stack renders them well; anything outside needs justification.

| Archetype | Student action | Renders with | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Parameter → consequence** | drags one quantity | Mafs | sectors, area/perimeter, gradient, transformations, graph shape |
| **B. Classify into regions** | drags items into zones | raw SVG + pointer events | number sets, quadrilateral hierarchy, factors/multiples |
| **C. Construct to satisfy a condition** | places points/lines to meet a constraint | Mafs | loci, coordinate geometry, plotting, constructions |
| **D. Partition / compose** | splits or combines a figure | raw SVG or Mafs | composite area, fractions, algebraic areas, surface-area nets |
| **E. Order on a line** | arranges values in sequence | raw SVG | inequalities, surds, directed numbers, data ordering |

State which archetype you chose and why the unit's content fits it.

### 3.3 The escape hatch — when *not* to build a sandbox

Some units have no manipulable object: symbolic manipulation, formal proof,
index laws, algebraic factorisation. **Forcing an archetype onto these produces
a widget that illustrates nothing.** If §3.1 cannot be written as a genuine
`change → consequence` sentence, say so plainly, and instead:

- reuse an existing component if one genuinely fits, **or**
- build a lighter **worked-step reveal** — a static SVG or KaTeX sequence where
  the student advances one algebraic step at a time, store-tracked — **or**
- build no sandbox, compensating with a longer, more finely decomposed exercise
  set in Part 4.

Choosing this path is a correct outcome, not a failure. State which and why.

### 3.4 Check for reuse before building

| Existing component | Covers |
|---|---|
| `<InteractiveSectorLazy/>` | circle sectors, central angle, arc length, live fraction reduction |
| `<RealNumberVennLazy/>` | drag-to-classify into nested set regions (archetype B) |

Reuse beats near-duplicate. If an existing component fits with a prop change,
propose the prop rather than a new file. If it *nearly* fits, say what would
have to change and let the reviewer decide — do not fork it silently.

### 3.5 Define the milestone

A milestone is a specific configuration the student must reach, validated by
the store (`checkMilestone` pattern). Derive it from the §2.4 misconception:
the milestone should be the configuration that makes the error impossible to
sustain — e.g. sweeping to exactly 180° to confront the semicircle halving.

Milestones are checked on **deliberate submission**, never continuously while
dragging. Free exploration builds intuition; the check is the pedagogical
moment. If the concept is genuinely exploratory with no single target
configuration, state `milestone: none` and explain.

## PART 4 — DERIVING THE EXERCISES

### 4.1 Source and progression

Build from the §2.4 exercise inventory. Prefer adapting the PDF's own
exercises — they carry the syllabus's difficulty calibration and phrasing
conventions. Write original questions only to fill a gap in the progression,
and say which are which.

Sequence: direct application → rearrangement/inverse → multi-step or composite.
4–6 questions. The last should combine this unit's concept with a §2.3
prerequisite, since that is what O/L structured questions actually do.

### 4.2 Decompose into micro-steps

Each multi-step question becomes a sequence of `ExamStep`s mirroring **the
stages a marking scheme awards marks for** — use the §2.4 mark pattern as the
model. One step = one markable stage. Do not merge two markable stages into one
step (it hides where the student actually failed), and do not split one stage
into two (it awards partial credit the real exam would not).

### 4.3 Hints target the step's specific error

Each step's `hintText` addresses the error *that step* invites — not a generic
restatement of the formula. A hint that repeats the rule teaches nothing at the
moment the student is stuck. Where a step corresponds to the §2.4 misconception,
the hint should name that misconception directly.

### 4.4 Answer tolerance

Derive tolerance from the PDF's own approximation convention (§2.1). If it uses
`π ≈ 22/7`, the validator must also accept a `3.14`-based answer, since students
use both. Compute the resulting divergence at this question's magnitudes, set
`epsilon` to cover it, and state that computation.

## PART 5 — LANGUAGE REQUIREMENTS

Write as a **native Sinhala / Tamil mathematics teacher**, not as a translator.

- Use the §2.1 extracted terminology verbatim. It overrides your own phrasing.
- Sinhala and Tamil versions are **parallel originals**, not one translated
  from the other. Each reads naturally in its own language; sentence structure
  may legitimately differ.
- Register: address the student directly and plainly, as a teacher would in
  class. No dense textbook paragraphs — this platform's pedagogy is
  explore-then-formalise.
- **Self-assess honestly.** Mark any specific line you are not confident reads
  as native — especially technical register, and everything inferred rather
  than extracted per §2.1 — with `// TODO: needs native speaker review`. Do not
  mark everything reflexively, and do not mark nothing to appear confident. The
  project greps for this string before release.

## PART 6 — ARCHITECTURE YOU MUST CONFORM TO

This project already exists. These are not suggestions — deviating breaks the
build or silently breaks the SEO/CLS guarantees.

### 6.1 Content pipeline (do not add registration code)

MDX lives at `content/<locale>/<grade>/<slug>.mdx`. `lib/content.ts` scans this
directory and drives `generateStaticParams`, `generateMetadata`, **and**
`app/sitemap.ts` from that one scan. Consequence: **creating the file is the
entire registration step.** Do not write route files, sitemap entries, or
metadata objects. If your output includes edits to `app/sitemap.ts` or any
`page.tsx`, you have misunderstood the architecture.

Frontmatter contract (all three keys required — `order` drives index sort):

```yaml
---
title: "«§2.1 extracted title, native script»"
description: "«1 sentence, native script, 120–155 chars — becomes the
  meta description and OpenGraph description verbatim»"
order: «integer from §1.1»
---
```

### 6.2 Exercises are structured data, not MDX

Exercises use the **`ExamDefinition`** type (`lib/exam-types.ts`), registered in
a `.ts` file under `content/paper-defs/`. They are *not* written as MDX prose.
This is load-bearing: `<StrictExamStepper>` consumes typed `ExamStep` data and
never accepts `children`, which is what structurally guarantees no ad unit can
render inside an active quiz. Do not break that by inlining questions into MDX.

Every user-facing string in an `ExamStep` is a `LocalizedString`
(`{ si: string; ta: string }`). **No English-only strings** in `title`,
`prompt`, or `hintText`.

### 6.3 Grading rules (already decided — implement, don't redesign)

- **3-strike hint rule:** hints surface after 3 incorrect attempts on a step.
- **Permanent hint tax:** once shown, `hintUsed` is sticky forever for that
  step. A later correct answer still scores
  `maxPoints × (1 − hintPenaltyPercent/100)`. Dismissing does not clear it.
  This mirrors O/L marking of assisted work.
- **Validators use mathjs** (`lib/answer-validator.ts`). Prefer
  `kind: "numeric-tolerance"` with `expectedExpression` written as a
  *mathjs-evaluable expression, not a pre-computed number* — e.g.
  `"22/7 * 7^2 / 2"`, never `"77"`. Set `epsilon` per §4.4.
- Do **not** use `kind: "algebraic-equivalence"` unless genuinely needed — it
  compares *evaluated numbers*, not symbolic forms.

### 6.4 Interactive component files

If Part 3 concluded a new component is needed, produce **three** files:

1. `components/interactive/<name>.tsx` — `"use client"`, the component.
2. `components/interactive/<name>-loader.tsx` — `next/dynamic` wrapper,
   `ssr: false`, exporting `<Name>Lazy`, with a skeleton whose `minHeight`
   **matches the real rendered height**. A mismatched skeleton is itself a CLS
   source. State how you derived the number.
3. `store/<name>-store.ts` — Zustand store owning **all** interaction state.

**Zustand owns the state; the render library is a controlled component.** Mafs
must never hold position/angle internally — it reads from the store and
dispatches back. This exists so the hint system and milestone validation can
read live interaction state. Follow `store/sector-store.ts` as reference.

Register new components in `components/mdx/mdx-components.tsx`.

### 6.5 Hard constraints

- **Heading hierarchy, no skipped levels.** The route renders the unit title as
  the page's only `<h1>`. MDX body starts at `##`. Never emit `#` in MDX.
  `<StrictExamStepper>` takes `headingLevel` — pass the level that keeps the
  chain unbroken where you place it.
- **MDX JSX props and `blockJS`** — ⚠️ *unresolved project issue.*
  `next-mdx-remote` v6 defaults to `blockJS: true`, which strips `{...}`
  expressions from MDX — the same syntax as JSX props like
  `targetDegrees={180}`. Until `lib/content.ts`'s `<MDXRemote>` call is
  confirmed to set `blockJS: false`, **prefer string props
  (`targetDegrees="180"`) or props defaulted inside the component.** Flag any
  prop you could not express this way.
- **Ad placement.** At most one `<AdBreak/>` per lesson, only between logical
  blocks — after the concept introduction, before the sandbox. Never between a
  sandbox's instructions and the sandbox. Never near the exercises.
- **Colour & contrast.** `#328E6E` (brand), `#245F4B` (dark — use for white
  text on coloured buttons; `#328E6E` fails WCAG AA at ~3.1:1), `#90C67C`,
  `#E1EEBC`, `#173229` (body), `#61736b` (muted), `#dce8dc` (borders).
- **Accessibility.** ARIA labels on every interactive control, `role="status"`
  on hint/feedback regions, `role="alert"` on validation errors, `sr-only`
  labels on inputs.
- **Strict TypeScript.** Explicit interfaces for all props and store slices.
  No `any`.
- **Maths rendering.** KaTeX via `<KatexInline tex="..." />`.

### Lesson body structure (MDX)

```
## «Hook — the concrete question this lesson answers»
   2–4 sentences. A real situation, not "In this lesson we will learn...".

## «Explore»
   1–2 sentences of setup, then the sandbox.
   <YourComponentLazy />
   Then 1–2 sentences directing attention: what should they notice while
   manipulating it? Point at the §2.4 misconception without naming the rule yet.

<AdBreak/>

## «Formalise»
   Now name and state the rule the student just observed. KaTeX for the
   formula. This ordering is deliberate — the sandbox builds intuition, this
   section names it.

## «Worked example»
   One example following the PDF's own solution conventions and notation
   (§2.1). Show the steps a marking scheme awards marks for.

## «Common mistake»
   Address the §2.4 misconception explicitly and show why it's wrong.

## «Practice»
   1–2 sentences framing the exercises, then the stepper.
```

If Part 3 concluded no sandbox (§3.3), replace **Explore** with a
**Work through it** section built on the worked-step reveal or static figure,
keeping the explore-before-formalise ordering intact.
